Song volume — levelled, then your own (approved)

Every song plays levelled by default: a quiet and a loud song sound equally loud. Each song's own volume moves it from there. The audio here is generated in the browser (synthesised loops at very different raw levels), so you can hear the levelling. Tap Listen, drag the slider, tap another song while listening to compare, and try Original file to hear what the song sounds like without levelling.

How it works

  • Levelled by default. Every song is brought to −14 LUFS (the streaming level), measured per ITU-R BS.1770 over the excerpt that plays, not the whole file — trimming re-levels at once, from loudness blocks stored once per file beside the beats.
  • Own volume: −12 to +6 dB in 1 dB steps, relative to the levelled default. The dashed thumb means "levelled" (like the automatic choices in the fade sections); "Back to levelled" resets.
  • Never distorts. The gain stops 1 dB below the song's loudest peak. The hatched end of the slider is out of reach; reaching it says so. A very quiet but peaky song (Song 3) may not reach the target at all — the hint says how far short it stays.
  • Just added (Song 4): measured in the background; until then it plays unlevelled, and your own volume still applies.
  • Same in the player and the export. One gain per song goes into the slideshow; the player multiplies it with the fades, the crossfade, the video duck and the TV remote's volume; the video export applies the same numbers. Clip sound keeps its own clip volume.
  • "Loudness of the songs" under the list is collapsed by default; opened, it shows each song's original level (grey line) and where it ends up.
