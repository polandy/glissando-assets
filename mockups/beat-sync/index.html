How it works

  • Detection on the device, once per music file, in the background (a worker, ~1.5 s per 3 minutes on a phone): the track is decoded, an onset curve built, the tempo found (50–200 BPM) and then each beat tracked, so a song that speeds up or slows down is followed. Kept beside the music like the automatic focus; a .glissando file does not carry it, the receiver listens again.
  • On the beat is the default once a beat is found. Each automatic picture change moves to the nearest beat (never below the 2-second minimum); a transition lasts a whole number of beats (within its usual 30 %, at most 1 s), so it starts on a beat and lands on one.
  • Own durations stay exact — a picture with its own duration keeps it, so the change after it can fall between beats (red in the lane); the next automatic change snaps again. The slideshow still ends with the music.
  • Evenly is stored with the track, like the excerpt and the fades, and travels with it.
  • No steady beat (speech, ambient, free tempo): the section says so and the pictures share the music evenly, as before. No choice is offered.
  • The same timing plays everywhere: player, video export, web page export.
Proposed (ADR-0021 in the PR): an own detector, no dependency. On generated test music it found the right tempo on all 8 beat tracks (beat F-measure ≥ 0.99, within ±6 ms) and reported "no beat" for an ambient pad; the libraries measured misread slow, triple-metre or accelerating music and found a tempo in the ambient pad.
