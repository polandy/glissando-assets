Portrait pictures — pairing and blurred fill

A portrait picture, cropped to cover a 16:9 screen, shows only 42% of its height — heads and feet go missing. New, automatic by default: two portrait pictures that follow each other pair up on one slide, side by side on a landscape screen, stacked on a phone held upright. A portrait picture without a portrait neighbour is shown whole over a blurred, darkened copy of itself. The images are public-domain photographs.

The problem

Today every picture is cropped to cover the screen. A 3:4 portrait picture, cover-cropped onto a 16:9 screen (TV, laptop, the video export), shows only 42% of its height — the rest is cut off the top and bottom. The same happens in reverse to a landscape picture on a phone held upright.

Three behaviours

  • Pair: two portrait pictures that follow each other in play order share one slide — side by side on a landscape screen, one over the other on a phone upright. Each half is cover-cropped (little loss, since each half's aspect ratio is close to the picture's own). A thin black gap (~0.6% of the long side) separates the halves. The pair is one slide, lasting 1.5× a single slide's duration; both halves zoom gently together (Ken Burns), no pan, and change together with one transition in and one out, so they move and change as a unit.
  • Blurred fill: a portrait picture with no portrait neighbour is shown whole (contain) over a blurred (~40px), darkened (brightness ~0.55), enlarged copy of itself filling the screen. Only the sharp front picture zooms gently; the blurred backdrop stays still.
  • Fill the screen: today's behaviour, unchanged — crop to cover, kept for anyone who prefers it or is used to it.

The rules

  • Pairs only form between neighbours in play order — pairing never reorders pictures.
  • A picture with its own motion, own duration or a caption (set in the picture editor) never pairs; it is always shown alone, with blurred fill if it needs it. The picture editor also has an explicit "Always alone" toggle, for a picture that would otherwise pair but shouldn't.
  • A pair is one slide, 1.5× the length of a single slide: timing and beat sync are unchanged per slide, whether that slide holds one picture or two. With music, the time is shared by weight (a single slide counts 1, a pair 1.5), and changes still snap to the beat.
  • Pairs are decided once, when composing the slideshow, so they stay the same on every screen. Side-by-side vs. stacked, and whether a single picture needs blurred fill, are decided by the player, per screen — so the video export (16:9) and the web page export see the same pairing, each laid out for its own screen.
  • The rule of thumb behind blurred fill: the player uses it whenever cropping would hide more than about a third of the picture; otherwise it crops as today. A 4:3 landscape picture on a 16:9 screen, for example, still crops as today (the loss is well under a third). That is symmetric — a landscape picture on a phone held upright loses most of its width to a crop, so it gets blurred fill too, and pairs of portraits stack vertically there instead of sitting side by side.

The setting

A slideshow-level setting, "Portrait pictures," with three options: Automatic (default — pairs plus blurred fill), One at a time (no pairs, blurred fill where needed), and Fill the screen (crop, today's behaviour, no pairs, no blurred fill).

Decided
  • Pair duration: a pair lasts 1.5× a single slide; with music, time is shared by weight (single = 1, pair = 1.5), beat snapping unchanged.
  • Per-picture override: an "Always alone" toggle in the picture editor, available now.
  • Pair transition: the two halves change together, as one slide.
  • Blurred fill threshold: used whenever cropping would hide more than about a third of the picture — e.g. a 4:3 landscape picture on a 16:9 screen still crops as today.
