Show a picture full screen in the picture selection

Import wizard, step 1 (also “Add pictures”). A thumbnail is too small to judge a picture — is it sharp, are eyes open, is it the right one? Tap or click a tile’s picture to look at it full screen; browse its neighbours; decide right there. Try it: click any picture below.

How it works

  • Open: the tile’s picture is a button (“View picture from <date>”, cursor zoom-in). Until now a tap did nothing; the ✕ and + keep their own targets and are not affected.
  • Full screen: the picture fills the whole screen, fitted (never cropped, unlike the tile), on near-black so colours are judged fairly. Top: ✕ close, the capture date and time, “3 of 12”. The bars hide on a tap on the picture (and come back on the next) so nothing covers it.
  • Browse: ← / → (keys, the side arrows on desktop, a swipe on the phone) go through the pictures of the strip in order; the set-aside ones follow in their groups. Ends do not wrap.
  • Decide on the spot: a strip picture offers Remove (same as ✕, same “Picture removed · Undo” toast; the viewer then shows the next picture, or closes when none is left). A set-aside picture offers Take in (same as +) and says why it was set aside (Similar / Blurry / Kept). Delete removes, Esc closes; focus returns to the tile that was open (or its successor).
  • Accessibility: a modal dialog (focus trapped, page behind inert, labelled “Picture from <date>”), 44 px targets, arrows announced as “Previous / Next picture”; no motion beyond a short fade, none with reduced motion.
  • Resolution: the picture is the 1600 px version already stored for the slideshow, not the 480 px thumbnail — loaded when opened, the neighbours preloaded. Offline, nothing extra needed.
  • Variant B adds a 1:1 toggle (Z, a double-tap/-click, or the 🔍 button): the picture shows at its real pixels and can be dragged, which is what sharpness and closed eyes need. Variant C adds a filmstrip of neighbours to jump around faster; costs vertical space, most useful on desktop.
Open questions
  • Variant: plain (A), with 1:1 (B), or with filmstrip (C)? Recommendation: B — judging sharpness is the point; the filmstrip duplicates the strip behind it.
  • Browser fullscreen: also request the browser’s Fullscreen API (hides the address bar on desktop; no effect on iOS)? Recommendation: no — the in-page overlay is enough and keeps Esc and the back button predictable.
  • Also in the slideshow editor? Same viewer for the editor’s strip would be a separate item; this one covers the import picture selection only.
