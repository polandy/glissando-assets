A playlist — several songs per slideshow

A slideshow can have several songs, played one after another. Each song keeps its own excerpt, fades and picture changes; the picture length and the hand-over between songs are the slideshow's. The music editor becomes the place to add, order and remove songs — also for a slideshow made without music. Try selecting, dragging (≡), removing and adding songs, the hand-over, the picture length, and a short versus a long slideshow.

How it works

  • Songs play in list order. Drag ≡ to reorder (or focus it and use the arrow keys); × removes a song. "Add a song" takes one or more files and appends them. The import's music step takes several songs the same way.
  • Per song, as today: excerpt (trim), fade in, fade out, picture changes (on the beat / evenly). Select a song to edit it; its waveform and listen buttons show above the panel's song section.
  • Per slideshow: picture length (about N s each / fill the music — now filling the whole playlist) and Between songs: Crossfade — the next song starts while the previous one fades out (an automatic fade there is 3 s instead of 2 s), or One after another — the next song starts when the previous has faded out completely.
  • Pictures on the beat: each picture change snaps to the beats of the song playing at that moment; a change inside a crossfade snaps to the incoming song.
  • When the playlist and slideshow differ (as with one song, ADR-0009): the music is heard until the slideshow ends and fades out there; songs that start after the end are not heard and are shown dimmed ("Not reached"). A longer slideshow plays its last pictures in silence.
  • Everywhere: player, video export, web page export, .glissando file and the server carry all songs. Beats are found per song in the background, as now.
Proposed data model. StoredSlideshow.music becomes songs: StoredMusic[] (each with its own trim, fades, picture changes) plus betweenSongs?: "sequence" (absent: crossfade). A stored slideshow with the single music reads as a one-song playlist. The player's JSON gets a list of tracks, each with where in the slideshow it starts; the player crossfades by running two audio elements at once.
