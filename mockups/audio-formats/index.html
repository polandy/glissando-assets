Import: music in formats the browser can't play

The import wizard's Music step. Pick a case: a track in Apple Lossless being converted on the device, the finished FLAC copy, a file that can't be used, a copy-protected one, or the empty drop zone. “Check device” tests which formats the browser you open it in can decode.

What the app does today

  • The Music step reads a file's duration with an <audio> element. A file it can't read gives the toast “The music could not be read” with Retry.
  • A file <audio> can read but decodeAudioData can't (rare) passes the Music step, then fails later: in the waveform (a generic error) and in the video export (“Video not created”).

What changes

  • Recognised by its first bytes: at import, Glissando recognises the format from the file's first bytes, not from its name or MIME type.
  • Portable music is stored as is: music every target browser plays (MP3, AAC, FLAC, Opus?, WAV) is kept unchanged.
  • Everything else becomes FLAC: ALAC, AIFF, CAF, AC-3/E-AC-3, WMA, AMR and Vorbis are converted on the device into a lossless FLAC copy, which every engine decodes — also on Safari, and also when the slideshow is later opened as a .glissando file or an HTML page on another device. Only the copy is stored.
  • Offline, off the main thread: the conversion runs in a worker; the WebAssembly decoder is part of the offline app, so nothing is downloaded at that moment.

Open questions

  1. Which files are converted? Everything outside the portable set (recommended — a slideshow made on an iPad also plays on Windows), or only what this browser can't play?
  2. How is a failure shown? As an inline panel in the Music step (recommended — it stays visible until the music is replaced) instead of today's toast.
  3. Which copy? FLAC (lossless, plays everywhere, about 5× the size of a lossy WMA or Vorbis original — a 4-minute track ≈ 25 MB) or Opus (small, but Safari only from 18.4).

Recommendation

  • Convert everything outside the portable set, so a slideshow plays on every device it is opened on.
  • Inline failure panel in place of the drop zone, with “Choose other music” and the formats that work.
  • FLAC copy: the size is the price of “plays everywhere” without a quality loss; revisit Opus once Safari 18.4 is the oldest supported.

Each sample is a generated 440 Hz tone, tried with decodeAudioData (what the waveform and the video export use) and with an <audio> element (what the Music step and the player use). “no answer” means nothing came back within 5 s.
